## Assignments

### Quizzes
5 quizzes administered on Blackboard, covering required readings. Must be completed by the posted deadline.

### Labs (45% total, 9% each)
5 labs, one per module milestone:

1. **Lab 1 — Reproducible Computing Setup:** Create a reproducible project structure with environment files and documentation. Record any AI assistance used.
2. **Lab 2 — Analysis Notebook:** Create an analysis notebook that accesses data, performs analysis, and creates a report communicating results.
3. **Lab 3 — Parsing Messy Health or Genomic Data:** Clean and structure raw data using both regex and generative AI, comparing accuracy and limitations.
4. **Lab 4 — Database-Driven Feature Table:** Construct a feature table using SQL and scripting, with clear metadata handling.
5. **Lab 5 — Scalable Analysis Workflow:** Create an analysis workflow that runs using a workflow management tool and produces presentable results.

Lab assignments contain additional tasks and questions for the graduate-level course.

- **PUBH 6854 (graduate):** all 5 labs, including the graduate-level tasks, are required for full credit.
- **PUBH 4201 (undergraduate):** labs are optional and may be completed for extra credit.

### Final Project (40% total)
Students may work in teams of 2–3. The goal is to use concepts in applied computing and data science to accomplish a real-world task (e.g., an analysis workflow, a software package/tool, or a database application). All projects must incorporate computation, AI assistance, and scientific oversight. **Project proposals are due Oct 7** and must be approved by the instructor.

Criteria for the final project:
- Reproducibility
- Code style and documentation
- Communication of findings — report/poster and presentation

| Component | % of Final Project grade |
|---|---|
| Project Proposal (due Oct 7) | 10% |
| Presentation | 20% |
| Report / Poster | 35% |
| Code, documentation, and other deliverables | 35% |

**Report vs. poster:**
- **PUBH 6854 (graduate):** a written report in a format appropriate to the project — typically a journal manuscript (other formats need instructor approval). For team projects, include a "Contributions" section listing each student's specific contributions. The report is required for full credit.
- **PUBH 4201 (undergraduate):** students prepare a scientific poster describing their project. Undergraduates may instead opt into the graduate-level manuscript-style report requirement.

## Workload

15-week course: ~5 hours/week independent learning (readings, assignment work, studying, group work) plus ~2.5 hours/week direct instruction. Total workload is at least 112.5 hours.

## Class policies

### Individual contributions and collaboration
Students are encouraged to discuss concepts, coding strategies, and data interpretation with peers; however, all submitted assignments, reports, and code must represent the student's own original work in their own words. Collaboration is permitted only at the level of conceptual discussion or troubleshooting.

- Sharing completed code, text, or analysis output for copying is considered academic dishonesty.
- For group exercises or case studies, all members must contribute meaningfully and document their roles.
- Any evidence of copied or jointly written individual work will result in an academic integrity review per GW's Code of Academic Integrity.

### Generative AI
Students are **required** to use generative AI — responsible use of AI is a key course objective. Submitted work must meet the following criteria:

- **Declare AI use.** Distinguish original parts of the work from AI-generated parts.
- **Maintain oversight and control.** Carefully review and edit AI output — it can be authoritative-sounding but incorrect, incomplete, or biased. Students remain fully responsible and accountable for the contents of their work.
- **Document the process**, not just the output — specifically: the LLM(s) used (e.g., GPT-5, Claude Opus 4.5, etc.), any agents used, and the key prompts, including prompts that produced off-target or incorrect results.
- **Respect task-specific restrictions.** Generative AI may not be used when a task or assignment specifically prohibits it. Part of the course involves comparing AI-generated work with student-generated work, so tasks marked as student-generated must not be AI-generated.

### Participation and discussion
Full participation credit requires more than attendance and on-time submission — it requires thoughtful, insightful, well-argued contributions and leadership in class discussions that demonstrate mastery of course material.

### Late work
Late work is accepted for any reason with a 10% grade reduction per day late. Extensions may be granted under extenuating circumstances with instructor approval (advance notice required); the instructor will work with the student on modified due dates/penalties. All late work must be submitted no later than 3 days prior to the grade submission deadline.

### Make-up work / make-up exams
Students who experience significant family or personal illness or an emergency after the final withdrawal date, and are unable to complete coursework, should ask the instructor for an Incomplete. Each case is handled individually, following the GWSPH Student Handbook Incomplete Policy.
